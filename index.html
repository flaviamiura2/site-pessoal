About

I started in numbers: a degree in actuarial science at USP, then seven years building the analytics layer for fintechs — Nubank, SumUp, now Musa. In between, I spent three years in Central America running finance and legal for an NGO, delivering leadership programs to young people from 18 countries. That's where I learned that a correct model changes nothing until someone decides to act on it. Today I lead the Analytics Ops team at Musa, across logistics, collections and operational margin — and the part I'm best at is the handoff between the two: making the number land with the person who has to own the consequence.

How I Work

Start where the work happens. Before modeling anything, I sit with the team that lives the problem. Most broken metrics are broken processes wearing a disguise.

A model nobody acts on has failed. Accuracy is the entry ticket. The work is done when someone changes a decision because of it.

Defend the margin, not the dashboard. Growth that costs more than it earns is a reporting problem waiting to happen.

Selected Work

01

Pricing calculator — Sales × Ops

Problem. Commercial was closing new clients on a fixed pricing model that ignored operational complexity: collection frequency, route cost, fleet capacity. Revenue grew; operational margin decayed.

Approach. Instead of blocking deals, I sat with the sales team to map how their pipeline actually worked, found the root cause in the pricing model itself, and built a calculator that priced each deal with its real operational inputs — fast enough to use live in a proposal.

Result. Operational margin improved materially in our largest region, and the lasting win was structural: sales and ops stopped negotiating against each other.

02

Portfolio acquisition due diligence

Problem. Growth by acquiring client portfolios skips the sales funnel, but it also imports whatever operational reality comes with those clients. My scope was to model OpEx for one portfolio and project margin and payback.

Approach. I recognized former clients in the list — accounts with a history of high operational complexity and negative margin. I pulled their historical measures and built two forecasts: one on the seller's data, one on our own history for those same accounts. The gap was the whole story.

Result. I recommended against the acquisition and brought the Sales and Ops directors along with the evidence. We traded short-term top line for a margin base we could defend — and the framework became how we screened portfolios afterwards.

03

Unstructured supplier data → automated OpEx close

Problem. Monthly OpEx closing depended on supplier reports arriving as PDFs, mismatched spreadsheets and photos of receipts. Normalizing them by hand consumed the hours that should have gone into variance analysis.

Approach. I built a prompting pipeline per supplier so an LLM would parse each format and output one consistent table, ready for audit against our internal numbers.

Result. Data preparation collapsed from hours to minutes, and the team's month-end work shifted from cleaning data to explaining it.
